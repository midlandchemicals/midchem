Seen from the commissioner's chair

The constraint isn't money.
It's credible provision.

Children with tracheostomies routinely wait weeks or months in intensive care after they are medically fit for discharge — not because funding is refused, but because the community market fails in three chronic ways. We designed Aria against each one.

Market failure 01

Capacity

The few specialist children's agencies are oversubscribed and decline packages at the acuity ceiling — precisely the children stuck longest.

Built-in answer

Children's tracheostomy and ventilation only — no dilution into generic homecare. Growth rate-limited by our own training throughput, so every package we accept is one we can staff.

Market failure 02

Fragility

Packages collapse when a provider loses two or three trained staff — bouncing the child back to hospital by emergency ambulance and destroying family confidence.

Built-in answer

5.5–6.0 WTE per 24/7 package plus a trained float pool at 15% of established hours — and a published no-hand-back protocol with joint escalation before any service change.

Market failure 03

Escalation behaviour

Under-trained community staff answer minor clinical change with 999 — converting a manageable event into an admission, and teaching clinicians to distrust discharge.

Built-in answer

Per-child emergency plans co-signed by the referring team, a 24/7 on-call registered nurse, and explicit criteria separating manage-at-home, contact-team and call-999 events.

The specification this model is built against

A named team, not a rota of strangers

Each package is staffed by a consistent team of complex care assistants trained to National Tracheostomy Safety Project competencies — every member signed off in emergency airway management through mandatory simulation before their first solo shift, and reassessed continuously.

Registered children's nurse case management

Every team is supervised by a registered children's nurse case manager holding a small, defined caseload — the clinical thread between the family, the team and the referring hospital.

Consultant-level clinical oversight

Formally contracted sessional medical leadership in paediatric respiratory medicine or intensive care — a governed appointment with declared interests, accountable for clinical policy, acuity ceilings and the interface with referring teams.

Static 12-hour shifts in one child's home

Zero travel downtime, deep familiarity with one child's baseline, and the roster efficiency that makes safe economics possible. Carers know the child — and the child knows their carers.

An escalation architecture that avoids ambulances

Per-child emergency plans agreed with the referring team, rehearsed monthly in the child's own home — including night-condition drills — with a registered nurse on call around the clock.

Hospital → home

The pathway home, step by step

No package is accepted until the full team exists. That discipline is why our packages hold — and why referring clinicians can trust the discharge.

1

ReferralAssessment against published criteria

Dependency-scored assessment with the referring team, using recognised paediatric dependency tools — acuity ceiling honestly applied. Entry begins with lower-acuity tracheostomy packages, building to full long-term ventilation as evidence accumulates.

2

Team buildSix people, recruited and signed off first

A full 5.5–6.0 WTE named team is recruited, trained and simulation-certified before acceptance. Families interview and approve the team who will live alongside them.

3

RehearsalSimulation in the child's actual home

Emergency airway drills run in the real bedroom, at night, before discharge — and monthly ever after. The family's own competencies are maintained as the second layer of resilience.

4

Discharge dayHome, with the hospital still in the loop

The first packages run with weekly joint review with the referring team. Escalation criteria are co-signed, not improvised.

5

SustainedNights watched, data reported

Package continuity, unplanned admissions, 999 activations, staffing consistency and family experience — reported quarterly, in the commissioner's own currency.

The no-hand-back protocol

Agencies hand back difficult packages; commissioners remember every one. Ours is published: service change only through joint escalation with the commissioner and referring team, with Aria bearing the cost of interim intensification. Package-continuity statistics are reported quarterly — and offered as a contractual KPI.
